Her Story

From the classroom to Parliament.

Harriet Nakwedde trained and worked as a teacher before entering public life. What followed was two decades of standing for office in Kayunga — through defeat, through renewed organizing, and eventually, in January 2026, through election as the district's Woman Member of Parliament.

A journey built on persistence

Twenty years. Four campaigns.

2006

First contested the Kayunga Woman MP seat

Ran on the FDC ticket. Unsuccessful.

2021

Two campaigns, two defeats

Contested Woman MP again and separately ran for Kayunga LC V Chairperson in a by-election, losing both. She was physically assaulted during the by-election campaign and pursued the matter through the courts.

Jan 2026

Elected Woman MP, Kayunga District

Twenty years after her first attempt. A petition against her win was filed and dismissed by the Kayunga Chief Magistrate's Court that same month, confirming her election.

May 2026

Appointed to opposition leadership

Named Deputy Chief Opposition Whip and Shadow Minister for the Presidency.

Kayunga

The place she represents.

She was born in Busaana Sub-county, Kayunga District, and it has been the constant backdrop to her career ever since — from her years as a teacher to two decades of building relationships across the district's sub-counties.

Priorities

What she stands for.

01

Rule of law & equal rights

A position she's held publicly since 2006 — respect for the rule of law, democracy, and people's rights without discrimination.

02

Girls' education

Keeping girls in school — including a campaign, described in a 2022 interview, to take pregnant girls back to school in Kayunga.

03

Roads & infrastructure

In 2023 she helped organize a Kayunga protest over an unfulfilled presidential pledge to tarmac a district road.

04

Electoral integrity

Assaulted during the contested 2021 LC V by-election, she pursued the matter through the courts rather than dropping it.
