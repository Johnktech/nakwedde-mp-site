Priorities

What she stands for.

Four commitments, each grounded in her own record — not slogans.

01

Rule of law & equal rights

A position she's held publicly since 2006 — respect for the rule of law, democracy, and people's rights without discrimination.

Explore priority

It is the thread running through nearly every other stance she has taken, from her handling of the 2026 election petition to her willingness to challenge alleged abuses through formal, institutional channels rather than outside them.

02

Girls' education

Keeping girls in school — a direct extension of her own career as a teacher.

Explore priority

In a 2022 interview, she described running a campaign to take pregnant girls back to school in Kayunga, reflecting a conviction that dropout is rarely permanent if someone is actively working to reverse it.

03

Roads & infrastructure

Holding public commitments to account when they go unfulfilled.

Explore priority

In 2023 she was among organizers of a Kayunga protest over an unfulfilled presidential pledge to tarmac a district road — demonstrators carried signs reading "No tarmac road, no votes."

04

Electoral integrity

Protecting credible democratic participation and political accountability.

Explore priority

She has described being physically assaulted during the contested 2021 LC V by-election campaign and pursued the matter through the courts rather than dropping it — the same instinct that shaped her response to the 2026 petition against her own election.
